# Agile Estimation Techniques

## Planning Poker™

This well-known method is commonly used by Scrum teams for small efforts (under 10 items). Planning Poker is consensus-based, meaning everyone agrees on the chosen number. Each individual has a deck of cards with numbers from the Fibonacci sequence (0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, etc.).

Planning Poker decks sometimes include coffee cup and question mark cards. The question mark card indicates a lack of understanding or information. The coffee cup card signifies a need for a break.

Planning Poker is used in Sprint Planning meetings. As each Product Backlog item/user story is discussed, team members lay a card face down. Then, everyone turns their card over at the same time, and the team discusses the estimates, particularly when they differ. Hiding estimates initially avoids bias, preventing team members from reacting to initial estimates.

## Dot Voting

Like Planning Poker, Dot Voting is good for sprints with low numbers of Sprint Backlog items. Team members start with colored dot stickers (S=green, M=blue, L=orange, XL=red) to represent estimated effort. Items or user stories are written on paper and placed around a table. Team members add stickers to the items.

## The Bucket System

The Bucket System is helpful for backlogs with many items since it can be done quickly (e.g., 200 items in one hour). It explores items in pre-determined "buckets" of complexity. These buckets are metaphorical; they don't require actual buckets. Note cards are used.

The team sets up a line of note cards down the center of the table, each marked with a number representing effort. The team writes each item or user story on a card and places it along the line. If a person doesn’t understand an item, they can offer it to someone else. Team members should spend no more than 120 seconds on each item.

## Large/Uncertain/Small

This quick method is great for product backlogs with similar items. It uses three categories: large, uncertain, and small. The team places items in one of the categories, then discusses and places more complex items until assigned.

## Ordering Method

Ideal for projects with a smaller team and a large number of Product Backlog items. A scale is prepared, and items are randomly placed ranging from low to high. Team members move any item one spot lower or higher, or pass their turn. This continues until members no longer want to move items.

## Affinity Mapping

Useful for teams with more than 20 items in their Product Backlog. A best practice is to use sticky notes placed on a wall, whiteboard, or table. Each sticky note features a different user story or item. The team discusses whether a sticky note is similar to the first item and places it in the same group or creates a new group.

After all items are grouped (3–10 groups total), the team gives a name to each group that represents the general theme. Then, the groups are prioritized by importance.

## Characteristics of Effective Estimation

- Avoids gathering false precision of estimates. Rough estimates are more accurate in Scrum.
- Avoids anchoring bias. Hiding initial estimates prevents team members from reacting to them.
- Promotes inclusivity. These techniques help teams develop trust and cohesiveness.
- Leads to effort discovery. Estimating in these dynamic ways can reveal strategies to complete items.

## Key Takeaway

There are several strategies to enlist when it comes to estimating effort and ordering your Product Backlog. Choosing a particular strategy is just a matter of what your team prefers.
