# Cue

* What are topology spread constraints in Kubernetes?
* How do they work?
* Why are they important?
* How can they be applied?
# Key Points
* **Definition:**
  * Topology spread constraints ensure **even distribution of pods** across a cluster’s topology.
* **How it works:**
  * Define rules for how many pods of a type can run on a **node, zone, or rack**.
  * Constraints can be **customized** to meet workload needs.
* **Benefits:**
  * Avoids **single points of failure**.
  * Improves **application resilience**.
  * Prevents **resource overloading**.
  * Promotes **balanced workload distribution**.
* **Implementation:**
  * Added via **Kubernetes API** or **command-line interface (kubectl)**.
# Summary
Topology spread constraints in Kubernetes distribute pods evenly across nodes, zones, or racks. By preventing single points of failure and resource overloading, they enhance resilience and ensure workloads are balanced across the cluster.
